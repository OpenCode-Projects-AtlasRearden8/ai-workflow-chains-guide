# Saving Chains You Reuse

*Turn a good run into a reusable tool — and meet the three house chains.*

## From one run to a permanent chain

Saving a chain isn't archiving; it's **harvesting**. After a run went well:

1. Copy the prompts you actually used (not the ones you planned to use) into
   the house template from chapter 3.
2. Replace your one-off details with `[BLANK]`s. If you had to look something
   up, that's a blank.
3. Mark each paste point `[PASTE FROM STEP N]`.
4. Write the `CHECK:` line per step — from memory, meaning it *must* be short.
5. Add a `WATCH OUT FOR` note for every seam that gave you trouble.
6. File it in your chain folder with a name you'll remember at 11 pm.

Now the next trip, purchase, or meeting starts with everything except the
thinking already done. A chain you've saved and re-run twice is worth more
than five clever one-offs.

## Version your chains like they're alive

After each run, one question: did anything in the chain fight me? Fix the
file. Some people add a tiny changelog at the bottom of the file:

```text
CHANGES
- 2026-10-12: step 2 now asks for 1–5 scores, not a total (totals felt overconfident)
- 2026-10-19: added "using only these facts" to step 3
```

Two lines per run, and your best chains quietly become the most-tested
prompts you own. If a change doesn't help after a run or two, change it back
— that's what the changelog is for.

## Share and re-mix

Because chains are plain text, they can be **traded like recipes**. A friend
or coworker can take your vet-a-purchase file, swap their own context into
the blanks, and delete a step that doesn't fit their situation. Nobody has to
install anything to use a chain — that's the local-first spirit of this
manual. Credit the chains you borrow from if you repost them; metabolize the
ones you improve.

## The three house chains

These are fully-worked starter chains in this manual, each in its own file,
each following the house template. Copy them, bend them, gut them — they're
meant to be starting points, not scripture.

### plan-a-trip — for planning any trip big or small

Turns "we should visit my sister this spring" into bookable decisions:
destination shortlist, a realistic day-by-day sketch, and a budget-to-
line-items pass. Its best trick: every step ends in something you can
*decide* about, not just admire. → [Run plan-a-trip](chains/plan-a-trip.md)

### vet-a-purchase — for any "should I buy this?" over about $50

Separates gathering facts from making the call, forces criteria *before*
looking at the product, and — the part people thank us for — asks the AI to
argue the case *against* the purchase. → [Run vet-a-purchase](chains/vet-a-purchase.md)

### prep-a-meeting — for walking into any meeting not-nervous

Works backward from the meeting's purpose to your two prepared sentences, a
question list, and the "after" note that captures what was decided. Built to
run the night before in about ten minutes. → [Run prep-a-meeting](chains/prep-a-meeting.md)

Notice the three chains share the same skeleton — goal-first slicing, facts
before judgment, a draft-then-check pass — because that skeleton is chapter
1's three moves with different costumes. Learning one well teaches the shape
of all of them.
