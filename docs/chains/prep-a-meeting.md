# Worked Chain: prep-a-meeting

*Walk in not-nervous. Ten minutes the night before, and one note for after.*

A complete, copy-and-run chain — the calm-before-the-meeting one. Works from
the meeting's *purpose* backward to the two sentences you'll actually say.
Privacy note: meeting topics are usually fine to paste; anything protected,
confidential to your employer's policy, or about a named person's health or
performance is not — describe roles, not names.

```text
=== CHAIN NAME: prep-a-meeting ===
WHAT IT'S FOR: being ready for a meeting where I need to speak —
               one purpose, two sentences, five questions, one afters-note.
WHAT YOU NEED TO START: the meeting invite context (topic, who it's
               with as roles, what I want out of it) — a few lines is
               plenty. Any scratch notes/scraps about my position.

--- STEP 1 ---  (model: any)
ASK: "I have a meeting tomorrow: '[BLANK: topic in one line]' with
      '[BLANK: roles, e.g. 'my manager and the scheduling lead']'.
      What I want out of it: '[BLANK: e.g. 'the duty roster changed,
      walk out knowing I'm not being reassigned without discussion']'.
      In 2 sentences: what is this meeting ACTUALLY for, from their
      side of the table? Then 1 sentence: what outcome would I call a
      win, stated as an observable fact."
CHECK: does the 'win' sentence name something someone else could
      verify happened? 'Make a good impression' is not observable;
      'a date on the calendar for the change' is.
PASTE INTO: step 2

--- STEP 2 ---  (model: any)
ASK: "Meeting purpose and my win: [PASTE FROM STEP 1]
      From my messy notes about my situation: [BLANK: scraps/notes]
      Write my opening, in MY voice: 2 sentences max, plain words, no
      jargon, no apology. Then list 3 questions I should ask, ordered
      so the most important comes FIRST. Format: the 2 sentences,
      then a 3-item numbered list."
CHECK: read your 2 sentences aloud — can you say them without
      stumbling? Rewrite any word you'd never say in person.
PASTE INTO: end of chain — you rehearse this, not the AI's version
      of it

--- STEP 3 ---  (model: your best available)
ASK: "Meeting purpose, win, my opening, and my questions:
      [PASTE FROM STEP 1 AND 2]
      Now be the person who has run 200 of these meetings: for each of
      my 3 questions, what is the MOST LIKELY answer and the one
      follow-up worth having ready? Also: name the single risk that
      I'd be wise to UNBRING (a topic better left out). Keep total
      under 150 words."
CHECK: is there advice you'd actually follow? Generic gab like
      'stay confident' — say 'specifics only' and rerun.
PASTE INTO: end — this is your cram sheet

--- STEP 4 (after the meeting) ---  (model: any, 3 minutes)
ASK: "Here's what I scribbled in and after a meeting just now:
      [BLANK: whatever you caught, typos welcome]
      Turn into: DECIDED (one line each) / NOT DECIDED / MY NEXT
      ACTION (owner: me, with a date) — nothing else. If something
      is ambiguous, list it under NOT DECIDED with a one-line
      question to ask."
CHECK: every item in MY NEXT ACTION would still make sense to you in
      a week. Fix vague ones now, while the room's still in your head.
PASTE INTO: end of chain — this is the point of the whole chain

--- WATCH OUT FOR ---
- Step 1 is the whole chain: if the win sentence isn't observable,
  every downstream step rehearses for a meeting that isn't happening.
- Step 2 in your voice beats step 2 eloquent. Stumbling words are a
  feature — you can say them out loud; you can't say-aloud elegance.
- Skip step 3 at your best model if material is sensitive — it's the
  nice-to-have, not the spine. Steps 1, 2, 4 alone are a complete
  meeting prep, fully local-in-your-hands.
- Never paste names, badge numbers, or protected topics. Roles
  ('shift lead') work equally well for every step.
```

## How it uses the manual's moves

- **Goal-first:** step 1 names the purpose and the observable win before
  any speechwriting — a meeting without a win sentence can't be won.
- **Draft-then-check:** step 2 asks for the AI's draft but its CHECK makes
  *you* the editor, in your own words, out loud.
- **Aftercare built in:** step 4 is the gather-then-shape move pointed at
  the *end* of the meeting — decided / not-decided / next action is the
  smallest honest meeting note there is.

## A worked run, abridged

Meeting: "shift scheduling discussion" with "charge nurse and staffing
coordinator." Want: "know my Thursdays aren't going away without a say."

- **Step 1 win:** "my regular Thursdays appear in the posted schedule for
  the next cycle, or I have a date when that's confirmed." Observable ✓.
- **Step 2 opening, after an edit-round:** "Before we go through the new
  grid — I'd like fifteen minutes on the Thursday pattern, because it's
  the piece I can't flex. Is it on the table?" (First question moved to
  the front, exactly as the chain forced it.)
- **Step 3 flagged** the likely answer ("the grid's already drafted, but
  swaps are handled in the coordinator's sheet") and one unbring: "don't
  open with the overtime grievance — that meeting isn't today." Sub-150
  words, all specifics.
- **After-meeting note** (step 4): DECIDED: grid posted Monday; NOT
  DECIDED: swap rules past week 2 — one question to ask; MY NEXT ACTION:
  confirm my four Thursdays appear on Monday's post, by Tuesday.

Ten minutes the night before. You walk in with two sentences and one
question already in your mouth. The nerves shrink to their real size.
