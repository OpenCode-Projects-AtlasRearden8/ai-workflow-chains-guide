# Worked Chain: plan-a-trip

*From "we should really take that trip" to a shortlist you can book against.*

A complete, copy-and-run chain using the house template from the chains
chapter. Sensitive-note first, as always: dates and destinations are fine to
paste; how much you earn, your health conditions, and anything about other
real people by name are not — this chain is built to work without any of it.

```text
=== CHAIN NAME: plan-a-trip ===
WHAT IT'S FOR: turning a vague trip idea into 2 concrete options and a
               realistic day-by-day sketch, without drowning in tabs.
WHAT YOU NEED TO START: your vague idea; rough budget in mind; dates
               that are flexible vs. fixed, written in one line.
--- STEP 1 ---  (model: any — a free chat app is fine)
ASK: "Here's my trip idea, stated messily: '[BLANK: your idea]'
      My rough budget: '[BLANK: budget in general terms, e.g. 'not
      luxurious' or 'budget matters']'.
      My dates: '[BLANK: e.g. 'any week in April, fixed start day']'.
      Before suggesting destinations, ask me up to 3 questions that
      would most change your suggestions — then wait for my answers.
      After my answers, give me exactly 3 destination-type options
      (e.g. 'small mountain town', not 'Boulder, CO'), one line of why
      each fits, one honest 'this might annoy you' per option."
CHECK: do 2 of the 3 options sound like a trip you'd actually take?
      If all three feel flat, answer their questions with more feeling
      and rerun.
PASTE INTO: step 2

--- STEP 2 ---  (model: any)
ASK: "Here are 3 trip directions: [PASTE FROM STEP 1]
      Pick the one I should actually plan first, in 2 sentences of
      reasoning. Then do NOT plan the trip yet — instead, tell me the
      5 things I need to know or decide about that destination type
      before looking at flights (season, transport, one 'the thing
      people get wrong' warning). Bullet list."
CHECK: does each of the 5 bullets name a decision you can make by
      looking something up?  Anything fuzzy like 'enjoy the vibe' —
      demand a version you can act on.
PASTE INTO: step 3

--- STEP 3 ---  (model: your best available)
ASK: "Here's what I need to decide/learn: [PASTE FROM STEP 2]
      Draft a realistic day-by-day sketch for a 5-day version of this
      trip, assuming: [BLANK: non-negotiables, e.g. 'one slow morning
      a day']. Rules: morning/afternoon/evening per day, one line
      each; mark each line [must-book] [cheap] [weather-dependent];
      end with the 4 things I should actually book or reserve."
CHECK: count the [must-book] marks — more than 4 booking chores for 5
      days means the plan is over-packed. Trim it back and ask for the
      trimmed version.
PASTE INTO: end of chain — this is the final answer

--- WATCH OUT FOR ---
- Seam 1→2: if step 2 starts planning before you've answered its
  questions, say 'wait — answer the questions first' and rerun. It
  usually means your step-1 answers were thin.
- Step 3 over-stuffing: best models love packing 5 days full. The
  [must-book] count is the tripwire.
- Prices & hours here are NOT facts. The chain decides *what to look
  up* — all prices, opening hours, and rules come from real sites
  before you book anything.
- Model switch: fine to run steps 1–2 on your phone's free chat and
  step 3 on a stronger model. If switching, use the restatement check
  from the switching chapter.
```

## How it uses the manual's moves

- **Goal-first slicing:** the messy "we should really go somewhere" becomes
  a goal before any planning (step 0/1).
- **Decisions before details:** step 2 deliberately refuses to plan the trip
  until the deciding factors are listed — gather-then-shape.
- **A final answer you can act on:** step 3's rules force bookable, tagged
  lines instead of a dreamy itinerary.

## A worked run, abridged

Input idea: *"some kind of spring trip, not flying, budget matters."*

- **Step 1 asked:** best month for flexibility? who's going, adults-only?
  scenic vs. city? Answered: May, two adults, "small city with a river,
  walkable." Options came back: riverside college town / canal-region
  small city / coastal town. The honest note on coastal: "shoulder season
  means some places still closed — you said you hate calling to ask."
- **Step 2 picked** the canal-region city: two sentences of why, plus five
  pre-flight decisions including "the thing people get wrong: the old
  center and the train station are across the river — check which side
  your lodging is on."
- **Step 3 drafted** five days with 26 lines, 5 tagged `[must-book]` —
  trimmed to 4 items (lodging, one dinner, one tram pass, one tour).
  Ends: the booking list. Done in about twenty minutes, one tab opened
  *after* the chain, for real prices.

Edit the blanks, run it, then save your own edited copy — after one trip,
you'll want next year's to start from the file, not from memory.
